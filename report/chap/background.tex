\subsection{Machine Learning Algorithms}

\subsubsection{Classification Trees (Information-Based Learning)}
Decision tree algorithms partition a heterogeneous training dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathbf{x}_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^N$ into increasingly homogeneous subsets. At each internal node, a candidate split on attribute $a$ with threshold $\theta$ divides the parent partition $\mathcal{D}$ into disjoint children $\mathcal{D}_L = \{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{D} \mid x_a \le \theta\}$ and $\mathcal{D}_R = \{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{D} \mid x_a > \theta\}$.

Splits are selected greedily to maximize impurity reduction. Under Shannon Entropy, the impurity of partition $\mathcal{D}$ over target classes $C = \{c_1, \dots, c_K\}$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    H(\mathcal{D}) = - \sum_{k=1}^K p(c_k \mid \mathcal{D}) \log_2 p(c_k \mid \mathcal{D})
\end{equation}
The Information Gain (IG) of a candidate binary split is the difference between parent entropy and the weighted sum of child entropies:
\begin{equation}
    IG(\mathcal{D}, a, \theta) = H(\mathcal{D}) - \frac{|\mathcal{D}_L|}{|\mathcal{D}|} H(\mathcal{D}_L) - \frac{|\mathcal{D}_R|}{|\mathcal{D}|} H(\mathcal{D}_R)
\end{equation}
Alternatively, the Gini Impurity metric measures the probability of misclassifying a randomly chosen element:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Gini}(\mathcal{D}) = 1 - \sum_{k=1}^K p(c_k \mid \mathcal{D})^2
\end{equation}
Decision trees produce explicit, rule-based decision logic and train in $O(M \cdot N \log N)$ time, with test query latency bounded by the tree depth $O(\text{depth})$.
